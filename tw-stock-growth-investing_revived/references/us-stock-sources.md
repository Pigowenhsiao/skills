# US Stock Sources

## Official Data Priority

Use primary sources first. Prefer this order:

1. SEC filings
2. Company investor-relations site
3. Official earnings release or shareholder letter
4. Exchange or market data provider pages

Do not anchor the analysis on media summaries when filings or earnings materials are available.

## Primary Sources

- SEC company filings search:
  - `https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/`
- SEC submissions API:
  - `https://data.sec.gov/submissions/`
- Company investor-relations pages:
  - use the company's official IR site for earnings release, presentation, and transcript links

## Core Metrics By Company Type

### Software and platform companies

- revenue growth
- gross margin trend
- operating margin trend
- free cash flow margin
- net revenue retention or recurring revenue quality when disclosed
- customer concentration if material
- valuation by PE, EV/FCF, or EV/Sales depending on profitability stage

### Semiconductor and hardware companies

- revenue growth
- EPS growth
- gross margin trend
- inventory trend
- capex direction
- customer concentration
- valuation by PE, EV/EBIT, or FCF where relevant

### Consumer, industrial, and heavy-asset companies

- revenue growth
- operating margin trend
- free cash flow conversion
- capex burden
- leverage and interest coverage
- valuation by PE, EV/EBIT, or EV/FCF

## Default Practical Filters

For US growth names, a practical default screen is:

- recent quarterly revenue growth is positive
- EPS trend is positive or inflecting upward
- free cash flow is positive, or the path to positive is explicit and near-term
- balance sheet is not stretched
- valuation must be explainable by growth durability and moat

Do not automatically reject a high-PE company if it has:

- clear moat
- strong recurring cash flow
- durable pricing power
- expanding margins

## Manual Second-Pass Questions

1. Is this a category leader or a niche leader?
2. What is the moat: brand, ecosystem, switching cost, network effect, regulation, scale, or proprietary data?
3. Does the company already have a stable cash-flow engine?
4. Is current growth supported by durable demand rather than one-off pull-forward?
5. Is valuation supported by both growth rate and business quality?

## Common Red Flags

- revenue growth but weak cash flow conversion
- repeated stock-based compensation dilution without operating leverage
- dependence on one customer or one policy tailwind
- capital-intensive growth without balance-sheet room
- valuation justified only by narrative, not by margins or cash flow
